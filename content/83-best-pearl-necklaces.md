# The Best Pearl Necklaces That Aren't Your Grandmother's

My grandmother's pearls live in a box I open twice a year, and for most of my twenties I treated them as an obligation instead of a piece of jewellery. A single strand, perfectly matched, exactly the sort of thing you're meant to wear to a wedding and nowhere else. It took a baroque pearl on a plain gold chain, worn with a t-shirt, to make me realise the pearl itself was never the problem. It was the styling.

Pearls have spent decades photographed on the same twinset, at the same string length, around the same neck, and the association has calcified into a joke before anyone's actually looked at what's on the market now. A pearl is not inherently formal. Irregular, warm-toned, hung off a fine chain instead of knotted into a matched row, it is one of the more interesting things a jewellery box can hold, and it suits a coat sleeve better than most stones do.

This is the edit I'd hand anyone who's inherited pearls they don't wear or wants a first pair that isn't a wedding prop: six ways to buy a pearl necklace now, ranked by how hard I'd defend the purchase, plus what to do with the ones already sitting in a box.

> *In Brief: Modern pearl necklaces work as a single pearl on a fine chain, not just as a matched strand. Freshwater pearls are the most versatile and least expensive; Akoya gives the classic mirror-white glow; South Sea and Tahitian are larger and rarer, with prices to match. Baroque, irregularly shaped pearls cost less than perfectly round ones of equal quality and read as more contemporary. An inherited strand is usually worth restringing, not replacing, and costs a fraction of buying new.*

## Know what you're actually buying

Four types cover most of what's on the market, and the differences aren't academic; they change how a necklace looks and what it costs. Freshwater pearls, farmed mainly in China, are the most affordable and the most varied, coming in white, cream, pink and lavender at sizes from roughly 3mm up. Akoya pearls, saltwater and mainly Japanese, are smaller and famous for a mirror-bright, classically white finish, the look most people picture when they hear the word pearl. South Sea pearls, from Australia, Indonesia and the Philippines, are the largest of the four, with a satiny, not mirror-like, glow and a price to match their size. Tahitian pearls, from French Polynesia, are the so-called black pearls, actually a range of charcoal, silver and peacock-green tones, prized for size and colour together.

Baroque means irregular instead of perfectly round, and it is where the modern styling argument is strongest. Round pearls are a small share of any harvest, so roundness carries a premium: pricing guides put the discount on a baroque pearl of equivalent quality anywhere from around 20 per cent to more than half. That makes it the better buy as well as the better look for anyone who wants a necklace that reads as chosen, not inherited. ## The edit, ranked

Ranked by how hard I would defend the purchase to my bank, on the usual scale: is it worth the mortgage payment?

### 1. The pearl on a chain

A single freshwater pearl on a fine gold chain does more for the "not your grandmother's" argument than anything else on this list, because it removes the one thing that reads as formal: the matched row. [Completedworks](https://completedworks.com/collections/pearls-necklaces), the London brand Anna Jewsbury founded in 2013 with her brother Mark, with a showroom in Marylebone, makes single-pearl pendants in recycled gold vermeil from around £125. If you want the same organic idea at statement scale, its Parade of Possibilities necklace runs baroque pearls into twisted strands of freshwater pearls around the neckline, at £525 in gold plate. It is the brand I'd point to first if the word pearl still makes someone think of a twinset.

**Verdict:** worth the mortgage payment.

### 2. The sculptural statement

[Anissa Kermiche](https://anissakermiche.com/), the Paris-born, London-based designer who studied at Central Saint Martins and worked in Hatton Garden before launching her own line in 2016, describes her work as modern-day pearls suited as much to dive bars as debutante balls, and the description holds up. Her pieces set pearls into bolder, more architectural gold forms instead of letting them sit quietly on a chain, and prices run from the low hundreds into four figures depending on the piece.

**Verdict:** worth the mortgage payment for the right piece; check the specific design against the price before committing.

### 3. The proper strand

If what you actually want is a classic strand, done properly, [Coleman Douglas Pearls](https://www.pearls.co.uk/) is the specialist to go to. Founded in 1989 by Chrissie Douglas, whose family were pearl specialists in Aberdeen in the 19th century, it now works from a London studio at Vox Studios in Vauxhall and covers freshwater, Akoya, South Sea, Tahitian and natural pearls, with consultations in person or by video that let you compare lustre and match properly instead of from a product photograph. This is the entry for anyone who wants a genuine matched strand and is prepared to pay for one properly sourced instead of mass-produced.

**Verdict:** worth the mortgage payment, and the right place to start if a strand is the actual goal.

### 4. The baroque, with real presence

[Yoko London](https://yokolondon.com/), established in London in 1973 and now showing at Selfridges' Wonder Room on Oxford Street, works across Akoya, freshwater, South Sea and Tahitian pearls and does baroque particularly well, setting irregular pearls alongside diamonds for pieces with genuine weight and colour. This is the buy for someone who wants a pearl piece that reads as a considered purchase, not an accessory, without going fully bespoke.

**Verdict:** worth the mortgage payment.

### 5. The one that actually is your grandmother's

Here is the twist in the title. If a matched strand is already sitting in a box somewhere in the family, the honest advice is almost never to replace it. Restringing an existing strand, checking the clasp, and having a jeweller confirm the match costs a fraction of buying new, and it keeps a piece with an actual history instead of manufacturing a new one. Coleman Douglas, above, will assess a strand that has yellowed or needs restringing. If you'd rather post it, [The Pearl Market](https://www.thepearlmarket.co.uk/pearl-restringing.htm) in Carmarthenshire cleans, restrings and knots on silk from £1.95 an inch plus insured postage, which puts an 18-inch strand at around £35 before postage. Fixes, doesn't renew, applies to pearls as much as to anything else in this house.

**Verdict:** worth the mortgage payment, because you already paid it, decades ago, in someone else's name.

### 6. The accessible entry point

For a first pearl piece with no commitment attached, [Missoma's baroque pearl necklaces](https://www.missoma.com/collections/pearl-necklaces) run from about £115 to £175, in 18ct gold vermeil or sterling silver with cultured freshwater pearls sourced through Responsible Jewellery Council-certified suppliers. It is demi-fine, not fine, and it is the right place to test whether the single-pearl styling actually suits you before spending real money on the version above it.

**Verdict:** worth it, as a way to find out what you actually like before you commit further up this list.

## Caring for pearls, inherited or new

Pearls are soft, organic and easily damaged by the things everyone puts on before jewellery: perfume, hairspray, and make-up all shorten their lustre if applied afterwards instead of before. Put pearls on last, take them off first, and wipe them with a soft cloth before storing them separately from harder stones that can scratch the nacre. A strand worn regularly benefits from restringing every few years, since the thread stretches and weakens with wear, which is precisely why the inherited option above is a restringing job, not a museum piece to leave untouched.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between freshwater and Akoya pearls?

Freshwater pearls are farmed mainly in Chinese lakes and rivers, come in the widest range of colours and sizes, and are the most affordable. Akoya pearls are saltwater pearls, mainly from Japan, generally smaller, and known for a classic, mirror-bright white finish with rose or silver overtones. Both are genuine cultured pearls; the difference is character and price, not authenticity.

### Are baroque pearls lower quality than round ones?

No. Baroque simply means irregularly shaped instead of perfectly round, and a baroque pearl of the same lustre, surface quality and size as a round one typically costs noticeably less, anywhere from around a fifth to more than half, because round pearls are a small share of the harvest and roundness itself carries a premium in the trade. Many contemporary designers choose baroque pearls specifically for their individual, non-uniform shape.

### Should I restring an inherited pearl necklace?

In most cases, yes, instead of replacing it. A jeweller can restring the strand, check the clasp, and confirm the pearls match, for a fraction of what a comparable new strand would cost; postal services charge from around £2 an inch. The result keeps a piece with real provenance instead of starting from nothing.

### How should pearls be worn to avoid damage?

Put pearls on last, after perfume, hairspray and make-up, and take them off first when undressing, since these products dull the nacre over time. Store them separately from harder gemstones and metals that could scratch them, and have a well-worn strand restrung every few years as the thread weakens.

### What's a reasonable price for a first pearl necklace?

A demi-fine baroque pearl necklace from a brand like Missoma runs roughly £115 to £175, which is a sensible way to test the styling before spending more. A considered piece from an independent London studio such as Completedworks or Anissa Kermiche moves from the low hundreds upwards, and a proper matched strand from a specialist such as Coleman Douglas Pearls is priced by consultation.
